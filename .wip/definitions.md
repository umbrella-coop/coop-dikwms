## Knowledge
Knowledge is the contextualized understanding, awareness, or skill acquired through experience, study, or reasoning, serving as the bridge between raw information and wise decision-making.

**Core Types**

* **Explicit Knowledge:** Codified, structured, and easily stored or shared information (e.g., user manuals, database entries, scientific formulas).
* **Tacit Knowledge:** Intuitive, deeply personal, and experience-based know-how that is difficult to formalize or articulate (e.g., body language interpretation, artistic technique).
* **Implicit Knowledge:** Unwritten practical knowledge that *can* be articulated and documented, but currently resides solely in human memory or habits.

**Related Terms & Distinctions**

| Term | Primary Focus | Distinction from Knowledge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Data** | Raw, unorganized facts or symbols | Lacks context, structure, or meaning. |
| **Information** | Processed, structured data | Answers "who, what, where, when"; lacks practical, actionable context. |
| **Insight** | Sudden, deep comprehension | Represents an intuitive breakthrough rather than a structured body of understanding. |
| **Expertise** | Applied mastery and execution | Focuses on high-level performance and practical capability over theoretical understanding. |
| **Wisdom** | Ethical judgment and long-term synthesis | Evaluates moral value, overarching principles, and the fundamental "why." |

## Hierarchy System

* **The DIKW Pyramid (Data $\rightarrow$ Information $\rightarrow$ Knowledge $\rightarrow$ Wisdom):**
* *Data:* Raw values (e.g., 100).
* *Information:* Contextualized data (e.g., 100°C water temperature).
* *Knowledge:* Actionable understanding (e.g., Water boils at 100°C, making it safe for sterilization).
* *Wisdom:* Ethical and strategic judgment (e.g., Determining when boiling water is the most resource-efficient method for purifying a community supply).

## Book: The Knowledge-Creating Company: How Japanese Companies Create the Dynamics of Innovation

Written in 1995 by Ikujiro Nonaka and Hirotaka Takeuchi, *The Knowledge-Creating Company* is the foundational text of modern Knowledge Management. It argues that competitive advantage stems not from merely processing information, but from an organization's capacity to continuously create new knowledge, convert it into products, and rapidly diffuse it throughout the enterprise.

**The SECI Model (The Knowledge Spiral)**

At the heart of the book is the SECI framework, which models how tacit (intuitive) and explicit (codified) knowledge continuously transform into one another across individual, team, and organizational levels.

| Phase | Conversion Type | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Socialization** | Tacit $\rightarrow$ Tacit | Sharing unspoken, experience-based insights through direct observation and joint practice. | Apprenticeship, shadowing, informal coffee chats. |
| **Externalization** | Tacit $\rightarrow$ Explicit | Articulating subjective mental models into tangible concepts, analogies, or specifications. | Translating a master baker's dough feel into mechanical design specs. |
| **Combination** | Explicit $\rightarrow$ Explicit | Sorting, combining, and reconfiguring formal documents and data into systemic structures. | Building a technical manual from project reports and data sets. |
| **Internalization** | Explicit $\rightarrow$ Tacit | Absorbing explicit knowledge into personal mental models and daily routines ("learning by doing"). | An engineer internalizing software principles by executing a user guide. |

**Key Organizational Enablers**

The authors identified five structural conditions that force knowledge out of individuals' heads and into the broader business ecosystem:

* **Middle-Up-Down Management:** Middle managers act as the critical core, translating top-floor strategic vision into ground-level operational reality.
* **Creative Chaos:** Intentional disruption or ambitious goals set by leadership that force teams to question existing paradigms.
* **Redundancy:** Deliberate overlapping of job responsibilities and information to encourage cross-departmental communication and alternative perspectives.
* **Requisite Variety:** Structuring internal teams to match the diversity and complexity of the external market environment.
* **Autonomy:** Granting self-organizing teams the freedom to experiment, fail, and cross-pollinate ideas without rigid oversight.

**Connection to Knowledge Frameworks**

Unlike static hierarchies like the DIKW pyramid, Nonaka and Takeuchi view knowledge as dynamic, social, and continuous. Knowledge is not a fixed asset stored in a static data store or graph; it is an active, spiraling process of conversion between lived human experience and formalized structures.
